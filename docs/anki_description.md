Featuring vocabulary for all stages of Japanese learning. The JLPT (Japanese Language Proficiency Tests) are certification of fluency for non-native speakers, and the official language test of Japan. It consists of 5 levels, with <b>N5</b> as a beginner friendly exam for Japanese Language, while <b>N1</b> is the hardest.

The deck is organised in order of advancing grade, making studying a particular grade very easy straight after downloading, no extra steps necessary! Each outer deck contains all the cards of the previous grade, plus those of the current grade. So by default, studying the N3 deck also includes all the vocabulary for N4 and N5 as well. The deck is split into subdecks as follows:

The flashcards are styled by default to include <b>clickable kanji</b>. Such kanji can be clicked to display the furigana (pronunciation) of the kanji.

<h1>Features</h1>

Japanese vocabulary ordered official language level. Suitable for all levels of study from beginner students to advanced native level.

Using a curated de-facto list of JLPT vocabulary, in use for over a decade. The same used by resources like <a href="https://jisho.org">Jisho.org</a>

An example sentence to go with each word. Demonstrating how the word is used in regular context.

<h1>Study advice for this deck</h1>

<i>Personal opinions and comments which may help get the most out of studying this deck.</i>

<h4>Don't study Recall cards for too long</h4>

The deck comes with "Recognition" (Japanese -> English) and "Recall" (English -> Japanese). Recall is great when starting to study, providing a bit of two way linking between new words. However, recall can quickly become frustrating and unclear. Consider yourself graduated from recall cards when you start to feel comfortable with the words, around N5 or N4.

Use Anki to either bury or delete the recall cards. (You can always re-include deleted cards by re-downloading the deck and merging it.) (See <a href="https://docs.ankiweb.net/editing.html?highlight=custom#customizing-fields">Customizing Fields</a> in the anki manual for more information.)

<h4>Don't worry about the sentences</h4>

Example sentence are included to help understand the context of the word. It can be hard to grasp the meaning of some words without context. For example, "trouble" in English could be anything from a physical altercation to a mental difficulty. The hope is that the provided sentences give some sense as to when and how to use more nuanced words.

The sentence vocabulary and grammar might be higher than the associated JLPT level, especially at the lower N4 and N5 levels. My hope is that these sentences are not off-putting to people new to studying, while also helpful to higher-level students.

Again, Anki has a wealth of customisation and it's easy to remove sentence information by removing the {{Example JP}} and {{Example EN}} sections of the card html.
